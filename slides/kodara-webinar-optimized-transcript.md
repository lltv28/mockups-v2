### Slide 3: Health and wellness interest online

Your audience is already online.

People are searching because they want to understand what is happening, what might be causing it, and what they should do next.

This chart is illustrative. It is not measured market demand and it is not a promise about any specific category.

What matters is the shape of the analysis.

We want to know three things. What outcome does someone want? How do they describe the cause? And are they looking for information, an approach, or help right now?

That is a much more useful starting point than saying, "I know a lot about hormone health," or, "I am a specialist in men's health."

Your topic tells us what you know. Their search tells us what they want today.

### Slide 4: Why taking expertise online can become more work

Now, you have probably heard some version of this before.

Take what you know and create an online course. Start a group program. Sell high-ticket coaching. Post every day on social media.

Any one of those models can work.

But most people on this webinar have not seen massive, reliable success from them. Or you looked at the amount of work involved and realized the offer, the content, the funnel, the sales process, the technology, the support, and the delivery would become an entire second business.

That is the problem.

You already have a real business to run. A second business is not freedom. It is another set of responsibilities competing for the same hours.

AI should remove work from your plate. If it creates another full-time job, it has missed the point.

### Slide 5: What we will build toward

We’re going to work through three things.

First, find the health questions people are already searching for. Then see how your expertise could become an online offer. Then see how AI can help market, sell, and deliver it.

The keyword demonstration is the first step in planning your “telehealth AI clinic.” We need to understand who it could help and what those people are looking for.

At the end, I’ll show you what Kodara builds and the next step if you want our help.

### Slide 9: Michelle’s story

My girlfriend Michelle was diagnosed with Graves Disease.

There were weeks when she had almost no energy, felt sick, dealt with severe joint pain, and spent an enormous amount of time driving to appointments around Southern California.

She would go through another appointment, another test, and another referral, and still feel like she did not have a clear path forward.

Watching someone you love go through that makes the problem very real.

The lesson I took from it was not that every doctor or every hospital was failing. There were smart people trying to help her.

The lesson was that people with complicated problems can spend months moving through a system while still searching for explanations, options, and people who understand what they are experiencing.

### Slide 10: Why Lucas built Kodara

Then I heard about someone with a similar experience who found a wellness expert online.

The expert described the problem in language that felt immediately recognizable, offered a virtual program, and made that expertise accessible from home.

That was the shift for me.

The expertise already existed. The person who needed it existed. The missing piece was a delivery model that helped them find each other.

So I focused the business on health and wellness and built the team behind Kodara.

The goal became straightforward: help qualified experts take what they already know and make it easier for the right people to access online.

### Slide 11: What clients have built

We have seen different versions of that with our clients.

Dr. Mike turned his sports-psychology expertise into an AI product and moved it into testing.

Martyn Buffler turned years of ideas and his cancer-survivor experience into a book and a first AI prototype.

Leanne Ellington already had a virtual neuroscience business, but it still depended heavily on her one-to-one time. Building a more automated path reduced that dependence and created room for a passion project she had wanted to pursue.

These are individual experiences. They are not promises about what will happen in every business.

The useful pattern is that each person began with real expertise and a real problem. The technology came after that.

### Slide 12: Healthcare context

Gallup reported that Americans' view of healthcare quality fell to a 24-year low.

That finding does not directly measure demand for independent providers, and I do not want to pretend it does.

What it does show is a growing gap in confidence.

When people are dissatisfied or cannot find a clear path through the traditional system, they often keep looking. They search online. They compare approaches. They look for credible people who can explain what they are experiencing.

That creates an opportunity for independent experts, but only if the expert can be found, understood, and trusted.
